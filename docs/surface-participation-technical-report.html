1. Executive summary

The branch implements a subgrid model for fabrication-sensitive surface loss. A practical device is solved on its usual zero-thickness-metal mesh. Small, fabrication-resolved coupon simulations are used to learn the local response of metal edges, nearby edge clusters, and three-dimensional vertices. Palace then replaces the unresolved thin-sheet response inside a matching neighborhood with the corresponding fabricated response.

Core principle

Keep the device mesh thin

The full-chip solve retains the geometry and cost of an ordinary thin-metal Palace model. Fabrication details are confined to reusable local coupons.

Numerical mechanism

Replace a local response operator

Fabricated-minus-thin Dirichlet-to-Neumann response matrices produce a low-rank correction to the electrostatic stiffness or Maxwell mass term.

Current status

Implemented, not yet production-qualified

Operators, automatic geometry matching, preflight, corrected solves, and diagnostics exist. General three-dimensional accuracy, process-library qualification, and practical-scale performance remain open.

What has changed relative to a prototype calibration script?

The branch now contains an integrated Palace response operator, automatic geometry classification, process-library selection, fixed-trace and fixed-flux closures, self-consistent electrostatic/driven/eigenmode solves, AMR-aware geometry caching, distributed trace evaluation, and confidence reporting.

Interpretation boundary.

“Implemented” means the code path and focused tests exist. It does not mean that every local geometry or solver combination has passed an independent fabrication-resolved accuracy study. The branch documentation explicitly classifies the feature as experimental.

2. The modeling problem

Interface dielectric loss is commonly summarized by a participation ratio pj for each lossy surface layer j. For a thin, perturbative dielectric film,

pj = Uj / Ue,     Qj−1 = pj tan δj. Uj is the electric energy assigned to the SA, MS, or MA interface layer, and Ue is the normalization electric energy, including lumped-capacitor energy where applicable.

A zero-thickness perfect-conductor sheet has a singular electric field at its perimeter. In the canonical thin-sheet limit, |E| behaves as r−1/2, so |E|² behaves approximately as 1/r near the edge. A surface trace cut off at mesh scale h therefore has logarithmic cutoff dependence, while the surrounding two-dimensional volume cross-section remains integrable:

Usurf(h,R) ∝ ∫hR dr/r = log(R/h),     Uvol(R) ∝ ∫0R |E|² r dr ∼ R. The second relation motivates the volume-annulus singular-amplitude diagnostic; it does not make the raw surface trace convergent.

Consequently:

  • raw SA/MS/MA participation may drift under refinement;
  • the mesh near the perimeter can dominate AMR;
  • the answer depends on an unphysical numerical cutoff;
  • resolving the real fabrication geometry everywhere is prohibitively costly.

Real fabrication removes the ideal singularity through finite metal thickness, substrate overetch, sidewall taper, and corner rounding. Those scales are often tens of nanometers, while a chip is millimeters across. Meshing both scales in a multi-qubit electromagnetic model produces an extreme and unnecessary scale separation if fabrication physics is local.

3. The response-correction idea

The method treats fabrication as a local constitutive response rather than as full-device geometry. The exterior device field supplies a trace on a contour or surface surrounding a metal-edge neighborhood. A coupon maps that trace to:

  • the electric energy stored in the local domain;
  • the SA, MS, and MA film energies inside the neighborhood;
  • the local flux or Dirichlet-to-Neumann response seen by the exterior field.

Two geometrically matched coupons are required: an ideal thin-metal coupon and a fabrication-resolved coupon. Their difference isolates the effect of fabrication while preserving the same exterior trace space. The response can then be reused at every compatible location in a device made with the same process.

1 · Thin device Ordinary Palace mesh with typed SA/MS/MA interfaces.
2 · Preflight Extract and classify every local edge neighborhood.
3 · Process library Retrieve or generate qualified thin/fabricated coupons.
4 · Corrected solve Apply local response patches without remeshing the device.
5 · Evidence Report raw, corrected, coverage, closure, and confidence data.

Locality is a hypothesis that must be checked.

A straight isolated-edge coupon is not valid near a narrow gap, corner, branch, nearby layer, or strongly curved edge. The implementation therefore classifies coupled neighborhoods and can fail closed with UnmatchedPolicy: "Error". Warning mode may omit a local neighborhood or retain a documented straight-edge fallback; reduced coverage and confidence mark that result as unqualified.

4. Mathematical formulation

4.1 Coupon trace basis and energy matrices

Let {φi} be basis traces on the matching contour ΓR. A local boundary trace is represented by coefficients a:

VΓ(a) = Σi ai φi.

Palace solves one electrostatic coupon problem per basis trace and obtains cross terms from summed fields. The resulting symmetric response matrices satisfy

Udomain(a) = aT Qdomain a,     Uj(a) = aT Qj a. For complex Maxwell traces, the quadratic forms are Hermitian. Separate matrices are stored for the thin and fabricated coupons and for each material interface.

Because electrostatic energy is one half of the boundary flux pairing, the local Dirichlet-to-Neumann energy operator is S = 2Qdomain.

4.2 Global low-rank correction

Let P map the global finite-element field to each coupon trace, including the subtraction of a conductor reference and any independent conductor-voltage states. The fabrication-response defect is

R = PT(Sfab − Sthin)P.

The matrix is not assembled as a dense global operator. Palace evaluates the trace, applies small dense coupon matrices patch by patch, and applies the exact transpose trace map.

4.3 Electrostatic, driven, and eigenmode systems

Kcorr = Kthin + R. Electrostatic corrected stiffness.
Acorr(ω) = Athin(ω) − ω²R. Driven Maxwell corrected system.
[K + λC + λ²(M + R) + A2(λ)] Ecorr = 0. Corrected eigenproblem, with λ = iω. The form reduces to the corresponding generalized linear eigenproblem when damping and frequency-dependent terms are absent.

The raw thin operator remains the preconditioner. Raw and corrected solves are kept separate so enabling the feature does not silently alter historical Palace output or the raw AMR sequence.

4.4 Corrected interface energy

For a target interface j, the divergent or mesh-sensitive thin core is removed and replaced by the complete fabricated coupon response:

Uj,corr = Uj,thin,outside R + aH Qj,fab,total a.

The corrected normalization energy receives the matching fabricated-minus-thin domain response. Thus both numerator and denominator are corrected consistently.

4.5 Fixed-trace, fixed-flux, and self-consistent responses

Fixed trace

Apply the fabricated response to the trace reconstructed from the unchanged thin-device field. This is the local Dirichlet closure.

Fixed flux

Transform the trace on the active subspace by aN = Sfab−1 SthinaD before evaluating fabricated energy. This is the local Neumann closure.

Closure spread

The relative difference between fixed-trace and fixed-flux responses measures information that postprocessing alone cannot recover from the thin field.

Self-consistent solve

Solve the globally coupled corrected system. This supplies exterior coupling rather than choosing one local postprocessing closure.

5. Implementation architecture

5.1 Configuration surfaces

Electrostatic correction is configured under Solver.Electrostatic.ResponseCorrection. Driven, eigenmode, and boundary-mode correction is configured under Solver.SurfaceResponseCorrection. Maxwell correction requires an automatic process library; explicit model placement remains an electrostatic prototype/legacy feature.

5.2 Surface diagnostics and matching-radius resolution

Typed dielectric entries use AutomaticEdges, EdgeDistances, and optionally LocalizeEdgeEnergy. EdgeRefinement can resolve the matching tube before the first field solve and reduce the raw field-error weight inside the replaced core. Elements crossing the matching contour retain full indicator weight.

The largest selected edge distance for a corrected interface must equal the process-library matching radius. The device mesh need not resolve the fabrication scale, but it must resolve the contour and the exterior annulus used to couple the local model to the global field.

5.3 AMR behavior

Mesh-independent geometry classification is cached across AMR iterations. The finite-element point/line interpolation stencil is rebuilt on each refined mesh. The raw field supplies the AMR indicator; corrected fields and corrected SPRs are evaluated at every solve without redefining the historical raw sequence.

6. Geometry matching and process libraries

The process library is the scientific contract between fabrication calibration and device simulation. A version-3 library records the matching radius, fabrication stack, interface-layer properties, response matrices, trace bases, geometric signatures, boundary laws, and optional qualification metadata.

Runtime matching is not general qualification verification.

Palace validates structure, compatibility, geometry, interface layers, and boundary-law metadata, but it generally trusts the numerical matrices in the selected library. Convergence and held-out qualification are responsibilities of the preparation workflow and its preserved evidence.

6.1 Fail-closed matching

A model is selected only when topology, conductor ownership, process normal, interface mapping, boundary law, and geometric tolerances are compatible. Pair interpolation is bracketed; there is no extrapolation. Rounded-corner radius interpolation additionally requires an explicitly passed held-out qualification span. Exact spatial clusters are not interpolated.

6.2 Exact plan-view masks

Nearby nonparallel edges can underdetermine the actual conductor footprint. The classifier therefore clips metal-surface facets to the interaction neighborhood, canonicalizes the union boundary independently of source triangulation, and stores a PlanViewBoundary. Fabricated coupon generation tapers, rounds, and overetches physical boundaries while retaining artificial continuation cuts. Open, point-touching, or otherwise nonmanifold masks fail closed.

7.1 Raw-output invariance

The raw solution, standard surface-Q.csv, ordinary field files, and raw AMR indicator remain the thin-metal result. Corrected results are additional outputs. This separation is important both scientifically and operationally: it allows direct comparison and prevents an experimental model from silently changing established Palace behavior.

7.2 Corrected driven PROM

A shifted resonance may not be represented by a reduced basis constructed from raw thin-metal snapshots. The adaptive driven implementation therefore constructs a second corrected PROM from corrected HDM samples and runs its own greedy convergence loop. The raw PROM continues to supply ordinary output and AMR.

7.3 Corrected eigenmodes

Raw and corrected eigensystems are solved separately. Corrected modes are paired to raw modes by a maximum-total-weight one-to-one assignment of normalized raw-mass overlaps. Corrected frequency and overlap are reported, with overlap acting as a confidence gate near crossings or mode reordering.

Boundary-mode limitation.

The current boundary-mode participation and response path uses the transverse electric field. The longitudinal component is stored separately and is not included in the corrected participation. The result is therefore intended for quasi-TEM modes; a quantitative longitudinal-energy gate is not yet present.

8. Confidence diagnostics and output

A corrected number is useful only if the local model covers the geometry and the reconstructed trace satisfies the current applicability diagnostics. Palace writes one confidence row per frequency/excitation, eigenmode, or boundary mode. These nominal gates are necessary evidence, not empirically calibrated accuracy guarantees and not a substitute for held-out coupon and device validation.

Primary outputs are:

  • surface-Q.csv: unchanged raw thin-metal SPRs;
  • surface-Q-corrected.csv: raw, fixed-trace, fixed-flux, and self-consistent values;
  • surface-response-confidence.csv: aggregate confidence diagnostics;
  • E_corrected_real/imag and optional D_corrected_real/imag: corrected fields;
  • surface-response-requirements.json: geometry-only preflight coverage.

Failed confidence does not suppress evidence.

Palace still writes corrected values when a gate fails, but warns that they are unqualified. Fixed-trace/fixed-flux closure failure does not by itself invalidate a self-consistent solve, because the latter supplies global exterior coupling.

A geometry-only preflight was run on the checked-in coarse single-transmon mesh at the reviewed snapshot. It is not a corrected field solve, but it measures how the classifier and planning workflow behave on a nontrivial device.

The empty process seed produced:

  • 22 requirement records and 215 missing occurrences;

  • 15 unique automatically preparatable coupon candidates after aggregation;
  • 10 exact spatial clusters, two isolated-edge mappings, one narrow-strip family, and one convex plus one concave corner family;

Canonical aggregation is working: repeated geometry can reuse one process model. The result also illustrates the calibration burden. 10. Current readiness and engineering gaps

Current verdict: suitable for controlled research pilots, not for production design signoff.

The major remaining work is no longer basic response-operator functionality. It is scientific qualification, reproducible process-library production, practical-device validation, and scalable execution.

10.1 Physical coverage gaps

  • Multi-edge, arbitrary-corner, smooth-curve, cap/branch, nonparallel, and cross-layer families are incompletely qualified.
  • Non-PEC response remains unqualified over frequency and boundary-law parameters.
  • High-order/AMR validation is incomplete for adaptive PROM and nonlinear eigenmode paths.
  • No realistic multi-qubit flip-chip reference satisfies the complete per-interface acceptance matrix.

10.2 Workflow and provenance gaps

  • The simple process-library builder can package solved but unqualified matrices as “complete.”
  • Current automatic qualifier defaults are not identical to the stricter roadmap release gates.
  • Unqualified finite-impedance candidates can leave top-level execution marked complete even though boundary-law physics is incomplete.
  • Cache keys do not yet include the Palace executable/commit and complete Julia/Gmsh dependency closure.
  • Qualification reports and remote staging are not yet a fully portable, content-addressed package.
  • The process-library schema and migration policy are not frozen.

10.3 Runtime scalability risks

Runtime patches and trace values are distributed, but geometry setup retains important replicated/global operations. Metal component construction gathers canonical surface vertices across ranks; cross-interface nearby-edge discovery includes global pairwise work; exact masks gather neighborhood facets; and the cached configuration retains the global patch layout.

Each corrected Krylov application currently performs host-side interpolation, dynamic buffer construction, MPI Alltoallv, dense local patch work, and transpose communication. This is likely to become latency- and synchronization-sensitive at high MPI counts or on accelerators. Practical corrected iteration counts and response-aware preconditioning have not been benchmarked.

10.4 Output and component attribution

Corrected output is naturally aggregated by dielectric-interface index. It does not carry stable CAD-semantic ownership such as qubit, readout line, resonator, coupler, pad, bump group, or junction lead. Connectivity-based edge component and chain identifiers are useful diagnostics but are not stable design identifiers.

Large local-edge CSV tables are also held in memory and fully rewritten. A realistic frequency sweep with component-level output needs streaming or a compact binary/sharded format.

11. Workflow for a realistic flip-chip pilot

A credible first pilot should deliberately constrain physics and geometry while exercising the complete production workflow. The purpose is to discover whether the process-library and runtime scaling assumptions hold before attempting a many-qubit design.

11.1 Recommended first scope

  • PEC metals;
  • linear undamped eigenmode or uniform driven solver;
  • one fabrication process per chip layer;
  • a two-chip subset containing one or two qubits, a readout/coupler, and representative bump-adjacent geometry;
  • no acceptance claim based on finite-impedance or HYBRID nonlinear results.

11.2 CAD-to-mesh contract

The thin device mesh should provide:

  • separate L1 and L2 SA, MS, and MA interface attributes;
  • correct metal boundary groups and explicit boundary laws;
  • artificial/truncation attributes that can be excluded from physical perimeters;
  • high-order curves or explicit sharp/smooth geometry metadata;
  • stable semantic component IDs if per-qubit or per-line SPR is required;
  • a documented division between planar response correction, finite bump geometry, junction leads, and the lumped Josephson barrier.

Spatial coupon generation currently assumes parallel or antiparallel fabrication planes. Cross-layer and bump-adjacent neighborhoods must therefore be inspected explicitly in preflight rather than assumed to be covered by planar edge models.

11.4 Preflight and coupon preparation

# 1. Classify the exact device mesh without solving fields.
build/bin/palace --surface-response-preflight device-preflight.json

# 2. Convert missing signatures into a deterministic coupon plan.
python3 examples/cpw2d/prepare_surface_response_coupons.py \
  postpro/device/surface-response-requirements.json \
  --output /work/process-coupons

# 3. Generate and qualify candidates. Use at least two FEM orders and
#    independent fixed-order h-refinement for corners/clusters/spatial masks.
python3 examples/cpw2d/prepare_surface_response_coupons.py \
  postpro/device/surface-response-requirements.json \
  --output /work/process-coupons \
  --execute \
  --palace build/bin/palace \
  --orders 2 3

# 4. Re-run preflight against the generated library and require Complete: true.
# 5. Run the device with UnmatchedPolicy: Error.

For production use, “automatically generated” is not synonymous with “qualified.” Review p- and h-convergence, held-out responses, boundary-law qualification, and the process provenance before accepting the library.

11.5 Acceptance ladder

  1. Geometry: preflight complete with strict matching and per-interface coverage of one.
  2. Coupon: all selected models pass final-order, independent h-refinement, and held-out gates.
  3. Contour: matching annulus resolved and corrected values stable with FEM order/AMR.
  4. Closure: fixed-trace/fixed-flux spread passes, or the self-consistent result passes every non-closure gate.
  5. Physics: representative fabrication-resolved subdevices agree within the chosen per-interface error target.
  6. Scale: raw/corrected runtime, memory, communication, and output remain within project budgets.

11.6 Required reporting for a pilot

  • separate L1/L2 and SA/MS/MA values—never only a global aggregate;
  • raw, fixed-trace, fixed-flux, and self-consistent convergence sequences;
  • all confidence columns and unmatched signatures;
  • process-library checksums and complete qualification evidence;
  • component-level conservation if semantic attribution is used;
  • preflight/setup/solve timing and peak memory at every scale step.

12. Recommended path to production

  1. Close correctness and workflow gaps. Make the test suite green, align schema and runtime behavior, make qualification status fail closed, and prevent unqualified libraries from being labeled complete.
  2. Freeze a reproducible process-library contract. Version the standalone schema, hash every matrix/basis/report, embed the Palace executable and dependency manifests, and support automatic remote staging.
  3. Qualify the geometry families encountered by real layouts. Prioritize multi-edge clusters, corners and bends, caps/branches, bump-adjacent masks, and cross-layer interactions from actual preflight manifests.
  4. Add semantic component attribution. Carry stable CAD component IDs through edge segments and response patches, and aggregate corrected energy by layer, interface, component, and geometry family.
  5. Make geometry setup and response application scalable. Replace replicated pair discovery with a distributed spatial index, batch mask requests, reuse communication buffers, avoid host synchronization, and balance patches by trace/matrix cost.
  6. Validate a scale ladder with fabrication-resolved references. Progress from CPW cells to one qubit, two-chip subsets, and a full pilot. Require independent convergence and per-interface accuracy at every stage.
  7. Promote realistic cases into CI and benchmark gates. Protect raw-output invariance, preflight determinism, AMR reconstruction, corrected PROM/eigenmodes, output conservation, and multi-node performance.

Most important near-term experiment.

Run strict preflight on an actual two-chip design subset, generate its complete coupon plan, and measure the number of unique exact spatial signatures. That result will determine whether the present local-model vocabulary is practical or whether geometry standardization and more general parameterized responses are needed before further solver optimization.

13.1 Proposed release acceptance targets

  • Final two coupon FEM orders: ≤5% matrix change and ≤10% worst probe-energy change.
  • Fabricated-minus-thin domain defect: ≤5% change.
  • Significant held-out material channels: ≤5% response error.
  • Fabricated and corrected device values: independently converged to ≤2%.
  • Corrected versus fabricated reference: ≤5% for every significant physical interface.
  • Postprocessing-only overhead target: ≤10% wall time and memory.
  • Self-consistent overhead target: ≤25% wall time and ≤15% peak memory.
  • No rank retains a replicated device-scale surface representation.

13.2 Scope exclusions

The method does not attempt a full-device fabrication-resolved mesh, a surface boundary-element formulation, or a microscopic Josephson-barrier model. Junction leads and nearby planar metal edges are in scope; the Josephson barrier remains a lumped element unless a separate model changes that contract.
